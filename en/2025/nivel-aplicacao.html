Chapter 6

Custom GPTs and AI Agents

Create custom, specialized AI assistants. Learn to develop custom GPTs, intelligent agents, and AI systems that work as expert consultants in specific fields. The era of custom AI has arrived.

⏱️ 50-65 min 📖 Reading 🎥 Videos coming soon

📚 Key Topics:

  • What it is: AI systems that can act autonomously to achieve goals—not just answer questions, but plan sequences of actions, use tools, and carry out complex tasks with minimal human supervision.

    Why learn: AI agents represent the evolution from passive chatbots to active digital collaborators—they can automate complex workflows and dramatically multiply your execution capacity.

    Key concepts: Autonomy, action planning, tool use, multi-step reasoning, goal execution, agentic AI.

  • What it is: The process of "retraining" an existing AI model with data specific to your domain, adjusting the model's parameters so it specializes in particular tasks or areas of knowledge.

    Why learn: Fine-tuning lets you create highly specialized AI for your business—with your organization’s unique language and knowledge—outperforming general-purpose models in accuracy and relevance.

    Key concepts: Transfer learning, parameter tuning, specific training data, model specialization, domain-specific performance.

  • What it is: A technique that combines language generation with retrieving information from specific knowledge bases—the model searches for relevant documents and uses that information to generate more accurate, up-to-date answers.

    Why learn: RAG lets you create GPTs that access up-to-date, specific knowledge without retraining the model—ideal for corporate documentation, knowledge bases, and information that changes frequently.

    Key concepts: Document retrieval, embeddings, vector knowledge bases, contextualized generation, up-to-date information.

  • What it is: Turning specialized GPTs into sustainable sources of income through models such as SaaS (subscription), pay-per-use (usage-based payment), or licensing—creating new businesses based on expertise and specialized knowledge.

    Why learn: Monetizing GPTs represents a significant opportunity in the digital economy—it lets you turn specialized knowledge into scalable income, with viable business models ranging from $29/month to $299/month per user.

    Key concepts: SaaS model, pay-per-use, freemium, licensing, recurring revenue, strategic pricing.

  • What it is: A technique for creating and optimizing prompts that maximize the effectiveness of AI systems—essential for getting better performance from language models like GPT through strategies such as chain-of-thought and few-shot learning.

    Why learn: Prompt engineering applies universally and has a low barrier to entry, allowing anyone to optimize AI results without programming or advanced technical knowledge.

    Key concepts: Prompt design, output optimization, chain-of-thought, few-shot learning, context engineering, AI communication.

  • What it is: The process of integrating AI models into applications and workflows through APIs, enabling intelligent process automation without having to build models from scratch.

    Why learn: Practical for real business implementation, bridging AI concepts and concrete business applications—it lets you add intelligence to existing systems quickly.

    Key concepts: API integration, workflow automation, token management, cost optimization, latency handling.

Chapter 7

The New Professions of the AI Era

Discover the emerging professions of the AI era. Prompt Engineer, AI Strategy Consultant, AI Ethics Specialist, and more. Salaries of US$ 100k-335k/year in careers that didn’t exist 3 years ago.

⏱️ 55-70 min 📖 Reading 🎥 Videos coming soon 📥 Career Roadmap

📚 Key Topics:

  • What it is: A professional who manages products that incorporate AI, bridging technical teams and business stakeholders, defining requirements, and ensuring AI products deliver real value.

    Why learn: One of the most in-demand and best-paid professions in the AI era—combines product skills with an understanding of AI capabilities and limitations, essential for companies incorporating AI.

    Key concepts: Product lifecycle management, technical-business collaboration, ML metrics, AI ethics, data strategy.

  • What it is: A professional responsible for ensuring AI systems are developed and used ethically, fairly, and transparently—auditing models for bias and ensuring regulatory compliance.

    Why learn: With AI regulations increasing globally and growing concerns about bias, this profession is becoming essential in organizations that use AI—it combines ethics, technology, and compliance.

    Key concepts: AI bias, algorithmic fairness, explainable AI (XAI), regulatory compliance, AI auditing, responsible AI.

  • What it is: A professional who specializes in creating and optimizing prompts to maximize the effectiveness of AI systems—the most accessible profession of the AI era, requiring no programming or advanced technical training.

    Why learn: Low barrier to entry with high market demand—you can start as a freelancer offering specialized services, with universal applicability across any domain or industry.

    Key concepts: Prompt design, output optimization, chain-of-thought, few-shot learning, context engineering, AI communication.

  • What it is: A professional who helps organizations navigate digital transformation by identifying AI opportunities, developing implementation roadmaps, and ensuring ROI on AI investments.

    Why learn: Strategic for companies undergoing digital transformation, with growing demand and high salaries ($120k-180k)—combines business vision with technical AI expertise.

    Key concepts: AI maturity assessment, use case identification, strategy development, change management, ROI and metrics.

  • What it is: A professional responsible for creating educational experiences that use AI to maximize learning effectiveness—instructional designers specializing in AI and adaptive learning.

    Why learn: Education is a critical sector undergoing transformation with AI; learning design is essential for successfully implementing intelligent educational solutions.

    Key concepts: Adaptive instructional design, integrating AI into curricula, learning data analysis, personalized experiences.

  • What it is: A professional focused on ensuring AI systems comply with privacy regulations (GDPR, LGPD) and use data ethically and transparently—a data protector in the AI era.

    Why learn: Privacy regulations are becoming increasingly strict; critical expertise for companies working with personal data—compliance is non-negotiable.

    Key concepts: GDPR, LGPD, regulatory compliance, consent management, data security, data ethics.

Chapter 8

AI-Powered Content Creation and Digital Marketing

Revolutionize your content creation with AI. Learn to produce text, images, video, and audio at a professional scale. Companies report a 60-80% reduction in time and a 30-50% increase in engagement.

⏱️ 60-75 min 📖 Reading 🎥 Videos coming soon 📥 Tools + Templates

📚 Key Topics:

  • What it is: Ability to create unique, personalized content for each individual customer at massive scale—AI analyzes behavior and preferences to generate messages and experiences tailored specifically to each person.

    Why learn: Mass personalization drives 30-50% increases in engagement and 25-40% increases in conversions—modern consumers expect personalized experiences, and AI finally makes them economically viable.

    Key concepts: Dynamic segmentation, personalized content generation, behavioral analysis, individual optimization, scalable one-to-one marketing.

  • What it is: Using tools like Midjourney, DALL-E, and Stable Diffusion to create professional images, graphics, and designs without requiring design or photography skills—making professional visual creation accessible to everyone.

    Why learn: Cuts production costs by 40-70% and speeds up creative cycles, enabling anyone to create high-quality visual content for marketing, presentations, and communication.

    Key concepts: Image generation, visual prompt engineering, integration with creative workflows, quality control, brand consistency.

  • What it is: Using tools like RunwayML and Synthesia to create professional videos with digital avatars or by generating video from text—removing the technical and financial barriers to video production.

    Why learn: Video is the format with the highest engagement, and AI democratizes production that used to be expensive and complex—it lets you create multilingual content at scale without a studio or equipment.

    Key concepts: Video synthesis, digital avatars, lip sync, automated editing, multilingual content creation.

  • What it is: Using AI to optimize content for search engines, including keyword research, structural optimization, and creating content optimized for SERPs—automating complex SEO analysis.

    Why learn: SEO is critical for digital visibility, and AI can automate ongoing optimization that would be impractical to do manually—essential for online competitiveness.

    Key concepts: AI-powered keyword research, technical optimization, competitor analysis, creation of content that ranks, performance monitoring.

  • What it is: Implementing intelligent chatbots that conduct natural sales conversations, qualify leads, and personalize e-commerce experiences in real time—24/7, in multiple languages.

    Why learn: Chatbots can qualify leads 24/7 and significantly improve conversions—essential for modern e-commerce and scalable customer service.

    Key concepts: Conversational AI, intent recognition, lead qualification, dynamic personalization, CRM integration.
