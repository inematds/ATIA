Chapter 1

The AI Tsunami - Understanding the Digital Revolution

We are living in an unprecedented moment in history. Artificial intelligence is no longer a futuristic promise; it is a present-day reality fundamentally reshaping how we work, live, and interact with technology.

⏱️ 45-60 min 📖 Reading 🎥 3 videos

📚 Key Topics:

  • What it is: Unlike earlier technologies with linear growth, AI is expanding exponentially—it starts slowly but accelerates dramatically, creating changes that seem to appear "out of nowhere".

    Why learn: Understanding the exponential nature helps you anticipate future changes and avoid being caught off guard by the pace of technological transformation.

    Key concepts: Moore's Law, accelerating change, exponential curve, multiplier effect.

  • What it is: The journey of AI from its beginnings (1950) with Turing and McCarthy, through the “AI winters,” to today’s revolution in generative models like ChatGPT.

    Why learn: Knowing the history helps put the current moment in context, understand why things are different now, and avoid past mistakes.

    Key concepts: Turing test, expert systems, machine learning, deep learning, generative AI.

  • What it is: The simultaneous combination of massive computing power (cloud, GPUs), advanced algorithms (deep learning), big data, and intuitive interfaces—creating an unprecedented synergistic effect.

    Why learn: Understanding this convergence explains why today's AI is fundamentally different from previous waves of technology and why this is a critical moment.

    Key concepts: Cloud computing, GPUs, big data, deep learning, synergistic effect.

  • What it is: Advanced AI tools (ChatGPT, DALL-E, Midjourney) are available to anyone with internet access, no longer limited to large corporations or specialists.

    Why learn: This democratization means you don’t need to be a data scientist to benefit from AI—but you do need to understand how to use these tools effectively.

    Key concepts: Universal access, no-code tools, accessible APIs, positive feedback loop.

Chapter 2

Digital Transformation and the New Job Market

AI is redefining the concept of work. Understand the statistics: 9 million jobs eliminated, but 11 million created by 2030. Discover the most impacted sectors and the skills of the future.

⏱️ 50-65 min 📖 Reading 🎥 3 videos 📥 Checklist
⚠️
Paradigm shift: 70% of professional skills will change by 2030

📚 Key Topics:

  • What it is: The fusion of emerging technologies (AI, IoT, robotics, blockchain) that is fundamentally transforming how we live, work, and connect, blurring the lines between the physical, digital, and biological worlds.

    Why learn: Understanding this broader context helps you anticipate changes in the job market and position yourself strategically to take advantage of emerging opportunities.

    Key concepts: Automation of cognitive work, technological convergence, exponential speed of change, transformation of entire industries.

  • What it is: The fourth generation of manufacturing, integrating cyber-physical systems, IoT, cloud computing, and AI to create smart, autonomous factories with adaptive production and real-time optimization.

    Why learn: Understanding Industry 4.0 is essential not only for those working in manufacturing, but for all professionals, since its principles are being applied in virtually every sector.

    Key concepts: Cyber-physical systems, digital twins, predictive maintenance, collaborative robots, adaptive manufacturing.

  • What it is: A skills model that combines vertical depth in an area of specialization (the vertical stem of the “T”) with horizontal breadth across multiple disciplines (the horizontal bar of the “T”), enabling interdisciplinary collaboration.

    Why learn: T-shaped professionals are more resilient to change, can adapt to multiple contexts, and are extremely valuable on multidisciplinary teams working with AI.

    Key concepts: Deep specialization, broad knowledge, versatility, ability to integrate across domains, adaptability.

  • What it is: The ability to understand fundamental AI concepts, critically evaluate outputs from intelligent systems, identify opportunities to apply them, and work effectively with AI tools, without necessarily being a technical expert.

    Why learn: AI literacy is becoming as fundamental as digital literacy was in the past decade—essential for virtually every professional, regardless of sector or role.

    Key concepts: Understanding capabilities and limitations, critically evaluating outputs, identifying bias, AI ethics, basic prompt engineering.

Chapter 3

Prompt Engineering - The New Language of the Future

Master the most valuable skill of the AI era: creating effective prompts. Learn advanced techniques like Chain-of-Thought, Few-Shot Learning, and more. Includes 100+ practical examples.

⏱️ 55-70 min 📖 Reading 🎥 4 videos 📥 Template + 100 Examples
💡
Prompt Engineer Salaries: US$ 100k - 335k/year. Market: $280M → $2.5B by 2032

📚 Key Topics:

  • What it is: A prompt engineering technique that instructs AI to explain its reasoning step by step before reaching a conclusion, similar to “showing your work” in math, significantly improving the quality and accuracy of responses.

    Why learn: Chain-of-Thought dramatically improves AI's ability to solve complex problems, makes it possible to verify the logic behind its reasoning, and reduces errors in tasks that require multiple steps of thought.

    Key concepts: Explicit reasoning, problem decomposition, logic verification, process transparency, improved accuracy.

  • What it is: A technique where you provide a few examples (usually 2-5) of the desired output type within the prompt itself, allowing AI to learn the expected pattern, format, or style from these concrete examples.

    Why learn: Few-shot learning is one of the most effective ways to get consistent outputs in a specific format and style without additional model training.

    Key concepts: Learning from examples, format consistency, pattern transfer, implicit specification, output quality.

  • What it is: A technique that instructs AI to adopt a specific persona, professional role, or particular perspective (such as "act as an experienced CEO"), activating relevant knowledge and adjusting the tone and depth of its responses.

    Why learn: Role-playing lets you access AI’s specialized expertise, automatically adjusts the complexity and tone of responses, and can significantly improve the relevance and usefulness of outputs.

    Key concepts: Specialized personas, knowledge activation, tone adjustment, multiple perspectives, professional context.

  • What it is: Prompt engineering focuses on how you instruct AI (the question or command), while context engineering focuses on what information you provide to AI (data, tools, memory). They complement each other: a good prompt without adequate context is limited, and too much context without a good prompt is inefficient.

    Why learn: Mastering both lets you create much more powerful AI systems—you’ll know both how to ask (prompt) and what information to provide (context) to get the best possible results.

    Key concepts: Instructions vs. information, RAG systems, memory management, tool integration, information architecture.

Chapter 4

Context Engineering - Creating Intelligent Ecosystems

Go beyond prompts: learn to build complete systems that provide AI with relevant information. Integrate data, tools, and memory for professional applications.

⏱️ 45-55 min 📖 Reading 🎥 Videos coming soon

📚 Key Topics:

  • What it is: An architecture that combines information retrieval with AI text generation—the system first searches a knowledge base for relevant documents/data, then uses that information as context to generate accurate, up-to-date answers.

    Why learn: RAG solves one of AI's biggest problems: outdated knowledge and hallucinations. It lets AI access up-to-date information specific to your company and provide answers with verifiable sources.

    Key concepts: Semantic search, external knowledge bases, grounding in facts, hallucination reduction, up-to-date information.

  • What it is: A technique that converts text into numerical vectors that capture semantic meaning, allowing the computer to “understand” that “puppy” and “dog” are similar, enabling searches by meaning instead of exact words.

    Why learn: It is the fundamental technology behind RAG systems, intelligent search, and contextual AI—essential for anyone who wants to implement systems that understand what users really want.

    Key concepts: Vector representations, semantic similarity, embedding models, vector search, vector databases.

  • What it is: A data structure that represents information as a network of entities connected by relationships, similar to how the human brain organizes knowledge, enabling AI to understand complex connections.

    Why learn: Knowledge graphs allow AI systems to make sophisticated inferences, uncover hidden relationships, and answer complex questions that require connecting multiple pieces of information.

    Key concepts: Entities and relationships, RDF triples, ontologies, logical inference, graph navigation.

  • What it is: AI’s ability to recognize when it needs to use an external tool and automatically call that function with the correct parameters, transforming AI from something that only converses into AI that can perform concrete actions.

    Why learn: Function calling transforms AI from a passive chatbot into an active agent that can perform tasks—retrieve real-time data, make precise calculations, and integrate with enterprise systems.

    Key concepts: Tool use, API integration, parameter extraction, agent orchestration, workflow automation, agentic AI.

Chapter 5

Business Process Automation with AI

Identify automatable processes, use no-code tools (Zapier Agents, Make.com), implement chatbots, and calculate ROI. Predictive analytics and automated decision-making.

⏱️ 60-75 min 📖 Reading 🎥 Videos coming soon 🛠️ Tools
✏️
Practical exercise: Map 5 processes in your work that could be automated

📚 Key Topics:

  • What it is: Technology that uses “software robots” to automate repetitive, rule-based tasks by simulating human actions in user interfaces (clicking, typing, copying/pasting), enabling automation even without APIs.

    Why learn: RPA is often the fastest and most cost-effective way to automate existing processes without rewriting systems, especially useful with legacy systems. With modern AI, RPA has evolved into intelligent automation.

    Key concepts: Software bots, UI automation, rule-based processes, legacy systems, attended vs. unattended automation.

  • What it is: Tools that let you create automations and applications through visual interfaces (drag and drop) without writing traditional code, democratizing development for business professionals.

    Why learn: No-code is revolutionizing who can create automation—instead of waiting months for IT, you can build solutions in days or hours. Reduces implementation time by 70-90%.

    Key concepts: Visual development, citizen developers, technology democratization, reducing the IT backlog, organizational agility.

  • What it is: Systems that not only perform tasks automatically but also use machine learning to predict future needs and take proactive action—such as predicting when a machine will break down and scheduling maintenance before it fails.

    Why learn: Predictive automation shifts organizations from reactive to proactive, reducing costs (preventive maintenance), improving customer experience (anticipating needs), and optimizing resources.

    Key concepts: Applied machine learning, predictive analytics, predictive maintenance, proactive optimization, data-driven decision-making.
